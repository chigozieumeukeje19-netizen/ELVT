Why you now have two rest days

This is the change from your last plan

You were training seven days a week with no day off at all. Thursday and Sunday are now genuine full rest days. They are programmed, not optional, and they are not there because you have gone soft.

Why, specifically for you

Your blood work came back excellent across the board with one exception: hsCRP, a marker of systemic inflammation, came back elevated. Training every single day with no recovery window works directly against bringing that number down. Your anti inflammatory food choices were doing one job while a seven day training week undid it.

You also have a knee overload history, and there is a baby in the house. Recovery is not a resource you currently have in surplus, so we stopped spending it faster than it arrives.

Four lifting days, one run, two full rest days. That is still a serious week for anyone.

Why the second run went, and not a lifting day

Your goal is to lean out and build. The lifting is what does that, so the lifting stays.

The runs were the highest impact thing in your week, and you were doing them at 108kg on a knee with an overload history. That is the session most likely to cost you something. Meanwhile your 10,000 steps every single day is already doing a serious amount of aerobic work quietly in the background.

One quality Zone 2 run plus 10K daily steps is plenty right now. When your bodyweight comes down and the impact cost drops with it, the second run comes back.
