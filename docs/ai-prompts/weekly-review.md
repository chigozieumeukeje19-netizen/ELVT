# Weekly review drafter

Drafts the Monday card: what to change, why, and the message to the client. The coach accepts, edits or rejects every line.

Generated by `npm run ai:pack` against the synthetic client below. Regenerate
it rather than editing it: the point of the file is that the prompt in it is
the one the portal sends.

---

## The client it was generated against

A forty-something recomposition client with a lumbar fusion, four training
days, no running, and a stated failure mode in week three. She is the
hardest of the eight synthetic clients to draft for, which is why the pack
uses her: a model that handles the spine flag and the week three pattern
well will handle the straightforward ones.

---

## What a good response looks like

The three decision rules are the whole job. This week has no flag and no
two week trend, so it proposes the one change her own words point at and
nothing else. A response that changes calories on the strength of a single
bad week is the failure mode to watch for.

The message is two to five lines, carries real numbers, asks one question,
and contains no dash.

```json
{
  "changes": [
    {
      "field": "step_goal",
      "from": "7000",
      "to": "6000",
      "reason": "Three of seven on steps for two weeks running, and both times it was a clinic day. A goal she hits on a bad day is worth more than one she hits on a good one."
    }
  ],
  "message": "Week three: four sessions out of four, which is the part that matters.\nSteps were 3 of 7 and both misses were clinic days.\nI am dropping the step goal to 6,000 so a bad day still counts.\nWhat would make the walk happen on a day a clinic overruns?"
}
```

---

## What the portal does with it

Nothing parses this one yet. The Monday card is assembled by the portal
from the frozen week, and the drafted changes reach it as `plan_changes`
rows in draft that a coach accepts, edits or rejects one at a time.

So the thing to judge when reading a response here is whether the three
decision rules were followed, not whether it would parse. In particular:
a single bad week with no flag and no trend should produce no changes at
all, and a response that proposes something anyway is the failure mode to
watch for.

---

## What it can never change

Nothing in a paste reaches the derived half of a Blueprint. Goal, duration,
training days, equipment, injury flags and every target are computed from the
intake answers by code, and the paste supplies prose only. A model cannot
change which movements a client with a spinal fusion is allowed to do, whatever
it returns.

The voice rules are checked and reported as warnings rather than as errors.
The coach approves every word anyway, and refusing a good draft over one dash
would teach them to work around the check.
